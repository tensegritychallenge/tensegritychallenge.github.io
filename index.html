01 / The mission

A soft landing is only
the beginning.

Future missions need landing systems that are lightweight, resilient, and ready for unpredictable terrain. Tensegrity structures offer a different answer: distribute impact through a network of tension and compression.

Your team works like a planetary robotics crew: three phases, one machine, and a structure that has to do the job of an airbag, a chassis, and an actuator at once.

01

Build

Integrate the structure, payload, onboard power, actuation, and control.

02

Land

Absorb impact while protecting the scientific payload.

03

Respond

Complete a visible shape change after landing; movement is the stretch goal.

NASA concept reference

One structure.
Landing + mobility.

NASA Ames developed SUPERball as a tensegrity robot concept that could absorb landing impact, protect a payload, recover in any orientation, and then roll across uncertain terrain. This is the big idea behind the challenge—not a conventional rover wrapped in a cage, but a robot whose structure is itself the landing system.

02 / The program

A 12-week path
to the final drop.

Short, focused briefings introduce each new skill. Most program time is reserved for building, testing, and improving the robot with your team. Exact dates are announced with the mission handbook.

  1. Week 01

    Launch

    Kickoff & Team Formation

    Open the mission handbook, meet your team, and turn the challenge into a first design concept.

    30-minute briefing
  2. Week 02

    Workshop 01

    Tensegrity Fundamentals

    Learn the force paths, prestress, and rapid-prototyping principles behind stable tensegrity structures.

    30-minute mini-lesson + build
  3. Week 04

    Workshop 02

    Actuation, Electronics & Safety

    Integrate onboard power, motors, encoders, wireless control, and safe bench-testing practices.

    30-minute mini-lesson + test
  4. Week 06

    Checkpoint

    Design & Mentor Review

    Show an early prototype, identify the highest-risk subsystem, and leave with a focused test plan.

    Short team reviews
  5. Week 07

    Guest lecture

    Planetary Robotics

    Connect your design choices to real landing, mobility, and reliability problems in planetary exploration.

    30-minute talk + Q&A
  6. Week 08

    Workshop 03

    Drop Testing & Recovery

    Plan repeatable drop tests, protect the payload, document failure, and verify post-landing actuation.

    30-minute mini-lesson + test
  7. Week 10

    Test support

    Open Testing & Mentoring

    Use the drop zone, troubleshoot with mentors, and complete the evidence needed for final decisions.

    Open build session
  8. Week 12

    Final event

    Expo & Mission Trials

    Complete the official mission test and present the design decisions, data, and iterations behind your robot.

    Mission trials + presentations

03 / The rules

Land. Protect.
Respond.

Every team is judged against the same five requirements. Final drop height, payload mass, and measurement thresholds will be published after pilot testing.

01

Untethered operation

Carry onboard power and control. Wireless commands are allowed; external power or control wires are not.

Required
02

Payload protection

Keep the standard payload secured and functional through the official baseline drop.

Required
03

Structural survival

Remain test-ready without external repair or reassembly; critical electronics and actuation must still function.

Required
04

Post-landing actuation

Within 60 seconds, complete one visible and repeatable shape-change cycle after landing.

Required
05

Budget compliance

Keep purchased components within the $500 build budget described below.

Required
Stretch goal 01

Move after landing.

Translate at least one body length without external contact, under remote or autonomous control. The final course definition is TBD.

Optional exploration: compact packing and controlled deployment.
