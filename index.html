01 / The mission

A soft landing is only
the beginning.

Future missions need landing systems that are lightweight, resilient, and ready for unpredictable terrain. Tensegrity structures offer a different answer: distribute impact through a network of tension and compression.

Your team will act as a planetary robotics crew. The goal is to create an untethered tensegrity lander that protects its payload through impact—then recovers, deploys, and demonstrates controlled locomotion.

01

Pack

Collapse into a compact, launch-ready configuration.

02

Land

Absorb impact while protecting the scientific payload.

03

Move

Recover after landing and navigate under onboard power.

NASA concept reference

One structure.
Landing + mobility.

NASA Ames developed SUPERball as a tensegrity robot concept that could absorb landing impact, protect a payload, recover in any orientation, and then roll across uncertain terrain. This is the big idea behind the challenge—not a conventional rover wrapped in a cage, but a robot whose structure is itself the landing system.

02 / The program

From first sketch
to final drop.

A guided 14-week build season combines practical workshops, expert feedback, iterative testing, and a public final expo.

  1. Phase 01

    Kickoff

    Meet your team, open the mission brief, and define your first concept.

  2. Phase 02

    Build foundations

    Explore tensegrity mechanics, rapid prototyping, electronics, and controls.

  3. Phase 03

    Test + iterate

    Run drop and mobility trials, analyze failures, and improve with evidence.

  4. Phase 04

    Final expo

    Complete the mission course and present the engineering behind your design.

Program includes

Kickoff · 3 hands-on workshops · guest lecture · testing support · final expo

03 / How you win

Performance meets
engineering judgment.

The strongest team will not simply survive the highest drop. It will build a capable, elegant system—and show the process that made it better.

01

Mission performance

Landing level, payload protection, structural integrity, stability, and repeatability.

02

Untethered function

Reliable onboard power, actuation, packing, deployment, and locomotion.

03

Engineering efficiency

Low mass, thoughtful material use, simplicity, and ease of assembly.

04

Design process

Systematic prototyping, failure analysis, experimental data, and iteration.

05

Team presentation

A clear design rationale, honest test results, and a compelling technical story.
