The mission: survive the drop, then move.

Future missions need landing systems that are lightweight, resilient, and ready for unpredictable terrain. Tensegrity structures offer a different answer: distribute impact through a network of tension and compression.

Your team works like a planetary robotics crew: three phases, one machine, and a structure that has to do the job of an airbag, a chassis, and an actuator at once.

01

Build

Integrate the structure, payload, onboard power, actuation, and control.

02

Land

Absorb impact while protecting the scientific payload.

03

Respond

Complete a visible shape change after landing; movement is the stretch goal.

The NASA concept: landing + mobility.

NASA Ames developed SUPERball as a tensegrity robot concept that could absorb landing impact, protect a payload, recover in any orientation, and then roll across uncertain terrain. This is the big idea behind the challenge—not a conventional rover wrapped in a cage, but a robot whose structure is itself the landing system.

The program: workshops, mentoring, and a final drop.

Robotics Workshop Series

Four hands-on workshops covering tensegrity fundamentals, actuation and electronics, safe bench testing, and drop testing and recovery.

Proposal & Team Budget Review

Before committing to parts, present your design concept and planned spend, and review both with the organizers.

Team Check-in

A brief weekly touchpoint to report progress, surface blockers early, and keep the build moving.

Expo & Mission Trials

At the end of the program, complete the official mission test and present the design decisions, data, and iterations behind your robot.

Instruction & Technical Support

Organizers and lab staff are on hand across the whole program for design guidance, fabrication help, and troubleshooting.

Design budget

$500

After proposal review, each team can receive up to $500 for building the robot — up to 10 teams.

Design Awards

Final designs are evaluated on untethered operation, payload protection, post-landing actuation, engineering efficiency, teamwork, etc.
